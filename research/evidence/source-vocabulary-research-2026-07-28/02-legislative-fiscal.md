- **Assigned source IDs:** C07, C09-C12, C15; T1-05-T1-09; T2-04,

## Decision

Only the Congress.gov subject vocabularies are strong candidates for a reusable legislative subject module. Most other resources in this scope are source-assigned labels, document or event types, identifiers, schemas, or accounting classifications. They remain valuable, but they answer different questions:

- **Topics** say what a document or activity concerns.
- **Document and event types** say what kind of artifact or lifecycle step it is.
- **Identifiers and organization codes** say which person, organization, program, account, or award is involved.
- **Accounting and procurement codes** say how money, industries, products, services, or transactions are classified.
- **Schemas and ontologies** say how to represent those facts and their provenance.

A RefSpec implementation should preserve all five kinds, with their source and
version, rather than merging them into one concept hierarchy.

The recommended pattern is:

1. Use Congress.gov **Policy Areas** as the broad legislative spine and selected **Legislative Subject Terms** as the detailed legislative module.
2. Preserve CRS, CBO, GAO, Lobbying Disclosure Act, Grants.gov, Assistance Listings, Federal Advisory Committee Act, and state-assigned subjects as **source-assigned evidence**. Crosswalk them to the canonical module; do not replace them.
3. Keep filing types, report types, bill versions, appointment types, finding types, award types, notice types, budget functions, object classes, and account codes as **deterministic metadata**.
4. Normalize entities with authoritative identifiers first: BioGuide ID, Congress committee system code, Federal Election Commission committee ID, Unique Entity Identifier, Commercial and Government Entity code, Assistance Listing Number, federal hierarchy codes, Treasury Account Symbol, and Legal Entity Identifier when available.
5. Use the W3C Organization Ontology and PROV-O to represent organization structure and evidence history. Neither is a topic vocabulary.

### Legislative recommendation

Build the legislative module from Policy Areas plus the topical subset of Legislative Subject Terms. Preserve original CRS bill assignments and add explicit mappings to:

- CRS Product Topics;
- CBO Topics and budget functions;
- state-supplied bill subjects;
- Library of Congress headings only where a reviewed mapping adds value.

Do not infer that two artifacts share a topic merely because they reference the same bill. A bill, amendment, committee report, testimony, transcript, and cost estimate can discuss different aspects of the same legislative record.

## Negative and unsupported findings

These findings are as important as the positive candidates:

1. **No maintained national subject vocabulary exists for state bills.** Open States explicitly preserves subjects assigned by each state. LegiScan aggregates them but does not make them a nationally authoritative thesaurus.
2. **No national controlled vocabulary exists for state lobbying filings.** Each state defines its own filing types, subject areas, thresholds, and reporting periods.
3. **No cross-state enacted-budget chart of accounts exists.** Census and NASBO provide comparison classifications, but states retain their own legal funds, agencies, programs, and account structures.
4. **No supported public Oversight.gov subject taxonomy or API was found.** The live report-type filter is useful metadata; it is not a subject vocabulary.
5. **No current machine-readable GAO thesaurus was found.** The 1998 GAO Thesaurus should not seed the canonical core.
6. **No public, separately governed CRS Product Topic vocabulary or assignment API was found.** Topics are visible on product pages and search facets and should be captured with the edition.
7. **The LDA government-entity constant list is not an organization authority.** Its live values contain obsolete and explicit non-use placeholders.
8. **CBO budget functions, OMB functions/object classes, NAICS, PSC, FAC requirement codes, and FedScope/PLUM codes are not document topics.** They are high-value typed metadata and ranking features.
9. **USLM, BILLSTATUS, GSDM, Open Civic Data, ORG, and PROV-O are schemas or representation vocabularies, not topical classifications.**
10. **Library of Congress name authorities do not by themselves establish real-world legal identity.** Use them as bibliographic identity evidence and retain the distinction defined by MADS/RDF.
11. **OpenOMB is not the authority for apportionments.** It can reduce retrieval cost, but every accepted value must retain the underlying OMB file and version.
12. **NIGP commodity codes were not recommended for federal procurement.** They are primarily a state/local procurement product and carry proprietary licensing; PSC and NAICS are the federal authoritative classifications in this scope.

## Implementation consequences

For each ingested artifact or observation, record:

- `raw_value`, `raw_label`, source URL, source organization, source record ID, retrieval time, and vocabulary/specification version;
- whether the value is `source_assigned`, `deterministic`, `mapped`, or `model_assigned`;
- the artifact level at which the assignment was made;
- the mapping method, reviewer, mapping version, and confidence for every crosswalk;
- identifier type and validity dates for every entity link;
- supersession/version relationships for notices, reports, products, accounts, and organization records.

Do not let a crosswalk overwrite a source term. A query may use the mapped canonical concept, but the result should still show what the source actually said.

## Bottom line

The evidence supports a small, governed semantic core and a large, versioned evidence layer. Congress.gov supplies the strongest topical starting point. The remaining official resources add precision because they identify the artifact, lifecycle stage, organization, program, account, transaction, and provenance. Treating those operational codes as subjects would make search less accurate and erase the distinctions needed to explain why a result matched.
