# "Unstated" placeholders: 12,463 rows, three different things wearing one label

**Ledger row under investigation.** `authority_type = 'unstated'` in
`output/registry-real-data-sources/unified-agenda-parquet/unified_agenda_legal_authorities.parquet`
— 12,463 rows the builder judged to be placeholders rather than citations, and
deliberately did not count as parse failures. The stated reasoning: "a field
saying 'Not Assigned' is not a citation anyone failed to read."

**Verdict up front.** The pool is not one phenomenon. It is three, and each
carries a different fact about the record:

| family | rows | what it actually is | is "unstated" the right verdict? |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **ellipsis `...`** | **6,873** (55.1%) | a **documented elision marker**: the agency checked "there are additional legal authorities not listed below" | **No.** It says "there is more here", not "there is nothing here". It marks citations that exist and were never published. |
| **Not Yet Determined** | 5,461 (43.8%) | a **sanctioned form checkbox** — authority not yet decided | **Yes** — correctly not a parse failure. |
| **None / N/A / Not Applicable** | 129 (1.0%) | **off-form free text.** The form offers no "None" for legal authority | **Yes as a parse verdict** — there is no citation to read. But the *value* is a real defect, not a lawful encoding. |

The ellipsis family is 55% of this row and is mislabelled. It is not a citation
the classifier swallowed — the classifier never saw one, and could not have —
but it is a **known, uncounted incompleteness** that the label "unstated"
conceals. Nothing about the current builder behaviour is a bug; the label is
what is wrong. See §7, *What this does not license*, before acting.

> **Revision note.** An earlier draft of this report attributed the ellipsis to
> a *publisher truncation* and judged the `None` family *lawful*. Documentary
> evidence obtained after that draft (the RISC Regulatory Information Data form
> and its instructions, §0 below) overturned both readings. The ellipsis is an
> agency election, not a publisher defect; and `None` is off-form, not lawful.
> The empirical measurements are unchanged — only their attribution is. Both
> corrections are marked in place rather than quietly removed.

---

## Method

- **Sampling.** Population sorted by `(publication_id, rin)`, then
  `random.seed(20260822)`, `random.sample(population, 10)`. **Seed 20260822**,
  reproducible.
- **Full source records.** For all 12,463 rows — not just the 10 — the whole
  `<RIN_INFO>` element was read out of the pinned publisher XML at
  `output/registry-real-data-sources/unified-agenda-editions/REGINFO_RIN_DATA_<publication_id>.xml`
  (`AGENCY`, `PARENT_AGENCY`, `RULE_TITLE`, `ABSTRACT`, `CFR_LIST`, the whole
  `LEGAL_AUTHORITY_LIST`, `TIMETABLE_LIST`, `RULE_STAGE`, `RIN_STATUS`). All
  12,463 were located. Two editions contain malformed bytes that abort a
  strict XML parse, so extraction chunk-splits on `RIN_INFO` instead.
- **Read-only.** No parquet written, nothing rebuilt, no source file edited.
- **External corroboration** via eCFR part-authority notes (§2.4) and
  reginfo.gov's own `eAgendaViewRule` pages (§2.6).

## 0. What the publisher actually instructs — the form that produces all three

Every value in this ledger row is generated by one artifact: the RISC
**Regulatory Information Data (RID) form** and its instructions, which agencies
fill in through ROCIS. Index:
`https://www.reginfo.gov/public/jsp/regform/download.myjsp` →
`RID_Form_Instructions_Fall_2025.pdf`.

**The field is mandatory, by Executive Order.** EO 12866 §4(b):

> "The description of each regulatory action shall contain, at a minimum, a
> regulation identifier number, a brief summary of the action, **the legal
> authority for the action**, any legal deadline for the action, and the name
> and telephone number of a knowledgeable agency official."

**What to enter**, from the RID instructions:

> "Provide the citation(s) of the legal authority for the regulatory action.
> For statutes, cite the United States Code (U.S.C.) whenever possible. If a
> law is not yet codified, cite the Public Law number and section, not the
> Statutes at Large."

**And — the sentence that resolves this whole ledger row:**

> "If the legal authority is 'not yet determined,' check the appropriate box.
> **If you choose to list only some of the applicable citations, you may check
> the box that indicates there are more citations. In this case, the printed
> Agenda will contain an ellipsis (...) at the end of the list of citations.**"

The form's literal controls:

```
LEGAL AUTHORITY  [ ] Not Yet Determined  [ ] Check here if there are additional
                                             legal authorities not listed below
CFR CITATION     [ ] None  [ ] Not Yet Determined  [ ] Check here if there are
                                             additional CFR citations not listed below
```

Three things follow immediately, and they set the verdict for all three
families:

1. **`...` is not a parse artifact, not a truncation, and not free text. It is
   a checkbox** meaning *"there are more applicable citations, and I chose not
   to list them."* The omitted citations were never entered into ROCIS at all.
   This **confirms** the elision reading — from the publisher's own instruction
   — while **refuting** the idea that the publisher lost them.
2. **`Not Yet Determined` is a controlled value**, supplied by the form, not
   typed by an agency.
3. **There is no `None` option for LEGAL AUTHORITY.** The form offers `None`
   for CFR Citation and for Executive Order — but *not* for legal authority.
   So every `None` / `N/A` / `Not applicable` in this field is **off-form free
   text typed into a citation slot**, which is what §4 rebuilds its verdict on.

**The field is never empty.** `LEGAL_AUTHORITY_LIST` is present and non-empty
for 100% of `RIN_INFO` records in every edition sampled.

Context: 241,701 RIN-editions in the corpus carry a legal-authority list;
12,463 (5.2%) carry a placeholder, across 3,465 distinct RINs. The classifier
(`states_nothing`, `citation_grammar.py:938`) is a **whole-value exact match**
against a 13-item sentinel set after quote-stripping and casefolding. The two
steps that run before it — `_normalize_dashes` and `_repair_whole_value_label`
(`citation_grammar.py:1323`) — rewrite only citation *labels*, and every repair
pattern requires digits, so neither can manufacture a sentinel out of a
citation. The classifier therefore **cannot** swallow a real citation: no
partial match, no prefix match, no reachable normalisation path. Whatever is
wrong in this pool is not over-greedy matching, and no row here is a citation
the parser mistook for a blank.

### The first thing the aggregate cannot see

Grouping by RIN-edition splits the pool almost perfectly along the spelling:

| | rows |
|---|---:|
| placeholder sits **alongside stated authorities** | 6,872 |
| placeholder is the **only** authority | 5,591 |

and that first number is, to within one row, the ellipsis count. Broken out:

| spelling | only authority | alongside real citations |
|---|---:|---:|
| `...` | 2 | **6,871** |
| every other spelling | 5,589 | 1 |

An ellipsis is essentially never alone. Every other placeholder essentially
always is. They are not the same kind of thing and the shared label hides it.

## 2. Family A — the ellipsis (6,873 rows). This is not "nothing stated".

### 2.1 It is literally the last element of the publisher's list

```xml
<LEGAL_AUTHORITY_LIST>
    <LEGAL_AUTHORITY>104 Stat. 714-726</LEGAL_AUTHORITY>
    <LEGAL_AUTHORITY>16 U.S.C. 620-620j</LEGAL_AUTHORITY>
    <LEGAL_AUTHORITY>110 Stat. 1321-1323</LEGAL_AUTHORITY>
    <LEGAL_AUTHORITY>28 U.S.C. 2461</LEGAL_AUTHORITY>
    <LEGAL_AUTHORITY>...</LEGAL_AUTHORITY>
</LEGAL_AUTHORITY_LIST>
```
(`REGINFO_RIN_DATA_202510.xml:14702`)

Of 6,873 ellipsis rows: **6,815 are the final element** of a multi-entry list,
58 sit mid-list, 2 are the whole list, and none appears twice in one list.

### 2.2 The 1995–1999 editions carried a hard 16-element cap on top of the checkbox

*(Attribution corrected — see the revision note. The measurement below is
unchanged and stands on its own; what changed is what it is evidence **of**.)*

Scanning every `LEGAL_AUTHORITY_LIST` in every pinned edition:

| edition | longest authority list in the whole edition | lists with exactly 16 elements | of those, ending in `...` | lists longer than 16 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 199510 | **16** | 27 | **27** | **0** |
| 199604 | **16** | 75 | **75** | **0** |
| 199610 | **16** | 63 | **63** | **0** |
| 199704 | **16** | 40 | **40** | **0** |
| 199710 | **16** | 41 | **41** | **0** |
| 199804 | **16** | 47 | **47** | **0** |
| 199810 | **16** | 34 | **34** | **0** |
| 199904 | **16** | 25 | **25** | **0** |
| 199910 | 58 | 7 | 4 | 19 |
| … | … | … | … | … |
| 202510 | 58 | 7 | 1 | 35 |

Across eight consecutive editions — 38,111 RIN entries carrying 124,197
authority rows — **no legal authority list anywhere exceeds 16 elements, and
every single 16-element list ends in `...`** — 352 of 352, zero exceptions. The
`CFR_LIST` in those same editions is capped identically at 16.

That is a hard ceiling of *15 citations plus a marker*, and it is not a
coincidence that could survive eight editions and 38,111 entries. From the
October 1999 edition onward it is gone: the longest list jumps to 58, and the
longest lists no longer end in `...`.

**What the ceiling is evidence of.** My first reading was that the publisher's
export truncated at 15. The RID form (§0) shows the marker itself is an agency
checkbox, so that reading was wrong about the *mechanism*. The most economical
account consistent with both facts is that **the RID form of that era offered
15 legal-authority slots**, and an agency with more filled all 15 and ticked
"there are additional legal authorities not listed below". The ceiling is then
a **data-entry** limit rather than an export limit — which is still a limit,
and still not something any agency chose.

I cannot prove which it was. **[Undetermined]** — no 1990s RID form was
located, and the RegInfo XSD is untyped with no enumerations, so it documents
nothing here.

**What survives either way, and is the point:** a list can only sit at the
ceiling *with* a marker if more citations existed. So each of those **352 rows
had more than 15 applicable authorities and only 15 were published.** That is
true whether the fifteenth-slot limit lived in the form or in the export, and
it needs no inference beyond the table above.

### 2.3 The 1999.10–2025 ellipses (6,521 rows) — how much is hidden

After the ceiling lifts, the ellipsis persists for 26 more years, and here it
is purely the checkbox: the pre-ellipsis entry count varies from 0 to 88, and
within a single agency-and-edition it varies too (630 agency×edition groups
with ≥3 dotted rules show varying counts; only 73 show a constant one, and
those 73 are almost all the 1995–1999 ceiling at 15). Variance that large is
exactly what a per-entry agency election produces.

So *what* the marker means is no longer in question — §0 settles it. The open
question is only **how many citations it stands for**, and that the corpus can
partly answer: for a RIN whose list carries a trailing `...` in one edition, is
there another edition of the *same RIN* whose list carries no `...`? Comparing
the 6,815 trailing-ellipsis rows:

| | rows | share |
|---|---:|---:|
| an unelided edition of that RIN exists and is **longer** → the `...` demonstrably stood for real citations | **447** | 6.6% |
| an unelided edition exists and is the **same** length → the election was later reversed | 790 | 11.6% |
| an unelided edition exists but is **shorter** (agency edited the list down; test says nothing) | 826 | 12.1% |
| no unelided edition of that RIN anywhere in the 60 pinned editions | 4,752 | 69.7% |

Where the hiding is measurable, the amount hidden is substantial: **median 3
citations, mean 5.2, 90th percentile 15, maximum 38.**

One caveat on the "same length" row, against my own argument: 170 of those 790
are ceiling-era rows, where a 15-element unelided list sits exactly *at* the
ceiling and so proves nothing either way. Restricted to the post-1999 era
the split is 444 longer / 620 same / 788 shorter / 4,611 uncomparable.

So the *quantity* hidden is: **measured for 447 rows, apparently nil for 790,
undetermined for 5,578.** Note what the 790 now mean. Under §0 they are not
evidence that the marker means nothing — they are entries where the agency
ticked the box and a later edition shows the same list without it. That is an
agency correcting (or contradicting) its own election, which is a
**provenance** observation, not a semantic one.

Two clean illustrations:

- **Stands for real citations** — RIN 1024-AD14 (NPS, *Delaware Water Gap NRA
  Commercial Vehicle Fees*): editions 200310 and 200404 list five authorities
  then `...`; edition 200410 lists **`...` and nothing else**, while its own
  timetable records a final rule published at 69 FR 57171. The agency ticked
  the box and listed nothing at all — a rule with a published final action and
  no stated authority whatsoever.
- **The election reversed** — RIN 1652-AA09 (TSA) carries the same five
  authorities plus `...` in editions 200304–200410, then editions 200504–200610
  carry exactly those same five with the `...` dropped. Same for RIN 1615-AA83
  (USCIS) at edition 201110. Either the earlier tick was wrong or the later
  untick was; the record does not say which.

### 2.5 The 58 mid-list ellipses are a separate, unexplained artifact

Concentrated in editions 199910–200110 (BIS 12, BOP 9, FCA 7, SEC 7, FDA 5),
these have entries *after* the `...`, at no consistent position:

```
199910  0694-AA52 [BIS]  ['18 USC 2510 et seq', '...', '30 USC 185(s), 185 (u)',
                          '42 USC 6212', '10 USC 7420', … 14 entries total]
```

Here the ellipsis is not terminal, which the checkbox reading does not explain:
a "more citations follow" flag should render at the end. Its meaning is
**undetermined** — plausibly a stale marker carried forward from a prior
edition and then appended to, but that is a hypothesis, not a finding. 58 rows,
and **none since 2010** — treat it as a closed legacy defect.

### 2.6 Every rendering carries the ellipsis — XML, HTML and print alike

The obvious hope is that the `...` is an artifact of the XML export and that
reginfo.gov's human-facing page holds the full list. **It does not.** Fetching
`https://www.reginfo.gov/public/do/eAgendaViewRule?pubId=…&RIN=…` for two
dotted entries returns exactly what the XML holds, ellipsis included:

| RIN @ edition | XML `LEGAL_AUTHORITY_LIST` | reginfo.gov HTML "Legal Authority" |
|---|---|---|
| 1615-AA83 @ 201004 | 5 citations, then `...` | the same 5 citations, then `...` |
| 0648-AF96 @ 199604 | 15 citations, then `...` | the same 15 citations, then `...` |

For 0648-AF96 the HTML block renders sixteen lines ending in a literal `...` —
the publisher shows the marker to the public, on the page a person reads,
thirty years later.

This is exactly what §0 predicts, and it is the strongest single confirmation
of the documented reading: **the ellipsis is faithfully reproduced everywhere
because it encodes an agency assertion, not a rendering limit.** A truncation
would differ between renderings. An assertion does not.

The printed Federal Register Agenda agrees. HHS Regulatory Agenda, FR doc
`2025-18328` (2025-09-22), RIN 0910-AI61:

> "Legal Authority: 42 U.S.C. 262; 42 U.S.C. 264; 42 U.S.C. 300aa-25; 21 U.S.C.
> 321; 21 U.S.C. 351 to 353; 21 U.S.C. 355; 21 U.S.C. 360; 21 U.S.C. 371; 21
> U.S.C. 374; **. . .**"

XML, HTML and print all carry the same one fact.

Two consequences:

1. **The incompleteness is upstream of every rendering.** The omitted citations
   were never entered into ROCIS, so they exist in no publisher artifact at
   all. The pinned corpus is a faithful copy of a record that is itself
   partial — the corpus is not at fault, and re-pulling would change nothing.
2. **There is no recovery route through the publisher.** Not XML, not HTML, not
   print. This is why §7 recommends naming the elision rather than planning to
   fill it.

## 3. Family B — "Not Yet Determined" (5,461 rows). Verdict stands, now documented.

**This is a form checkbox, and the data proves it independently of §0.** In
edition 202510 all 163 occurrences are byte-identical `Not Yet Determined` —
zero casing variants, zero spelling variants. Free text typed by 100+ agencies
does not behave that way. The historical record shows the control arriving:
1995–1998 editions carry lowercase `Not yet determined` and combined forms
(`12 CFR Not yet determined`); from edition 199810 onward it is uniformly
title-case. **[Inference]** consistent with a controlled value introduced
around fall 1998.

5,460 of 5,461 stand entirely alone; exactly one (RIN 0790-AG73, DOD Office of
the Secretary, edition 200004: `['10 USC 136', 'Not Yet Determined']`) sits
beside a real citation. The `CFR_LIST` of the same entry is also a placeholder
56% of the time — the two fields agree, which is what a genuine "we haven't
decided" looks like. Only 31% have any timetable entry with a real FR
citation, i.e. these skew toward rules that have not published yet.

Stage profile fits: 46% Proposed Rule Stage, 20% Long-Term Actions, 8% Prerule
Stage. **"Unstated" is the correct verdict for this family.**

It is nonetheless sometimes *stale rather than true*:

- **RIN 2126-AC67** (FMCSA, specimen 5) says `Not Yet Determined` in editions
  202310 and 202404, then `49 U.S.C. 13301, 14504a` in 202410 — and its own
  202310 abstract already said the fees are "established by 49 U.S.C. 14504a".
  The authority was knowable and stated in prose while the field said unknown.
- **RIN 2070-AD45** (EPA, specimen 6) says `Not Yet Determined` across nine
  editions spanning 2001–2004 while its timetable records nine *already
  published* Federal Register notices going back to 63 FR 63926 (1998). It
  resolves in 200504 to `PPA`.

That is a **provenance smell, not a parse question.** The field is honestly
reporting the agency's submission; the agency's submission is out of date.

## 4. Family C — "None" / "N/A" / "Not Applicable" (129 rows). Off-form, and a real defect.

*(Verdict reversed — see the revision note. My first reading called this family
lawful. §0 shows it is not, and the stage profile agrees.)*

All 129 stand alone. **The form offers no `None` option for LEGAL AUTHORITY.**
It offers one for CFR Citation and one for Executive Order — deliberately, and
not for this field. So every value here is agency-typed free text in a citation
slot, contradicting EO 12866 §4(b)'s requirement that each entry state "the
legal authority for the action."

### Why I first read it as lawful, and why that was wrong

The entries do skew toward things that are not codified rules, which is what
misled me:

- **Withdrawn or merged entries.** All five modern `N/A` rows. RIN 0694-AH03
  (BIS, 202204): *"Request for Public Comment on a Simplified Trade Application
  and Reporting System"* — `CFR: None`, timetable `Withdrawn 03/23/2022`. RIN
  0694-AI86 (202304): `Merged With 0694-AH55`. RIN 0605-AA61 (202310): `Merged
  With 0694-AJ35`. RINs 0694-AJ86 / 0694-AJ97 (202510): both `Withdrawn
  09/22/2025`.
- **Guidance and science-policy documents.** The EPA 2080-AA05…AA08 series —
  *Final Guidelines for Carcinogen Risk Assessment* and its siblings — carries
  `Not applicable` as authority and `Not yet determined` as CFR across many
  editions. These published as FR **notices**. USDA Forest Service
  0596-AB65/AB66 behave the same way.

**The excuse fails on the law.** A notice is not exempt from citing authority:

- **1 CFR 22.2** — "The authority under which an agency issues a notice shall
  be cited in narrative form within text or in parentheses on a separate line
  following text."
- **OFR *Document Drafting Handbook* §4.4** — "You must cite the authority that
  authorizes your agency to issue your notice."
- **1 CFR 21.40** governs codified material, **1 CFR 22.5** proposed rules, and
  **1 CFR 21.41(a)** puts accuracy of authority citations on the issuing agency.

There is exactly one real gap: a *Rules*-category document with **no regulatory
text** — a general policy statement or interpretation — is reached by neither
21.40 (nothing to codify) nor 22.2 (not a notice). **[Undetermined]** whether
any of these 129 is that case; nothing in the Agenda record marks it.

**And the stage profile refutes the excuse directly.** By `RULE_STAGE`:
Completed Actions 61, **Final Rule Stage 25, Proposed Rule Stage 22**,
Long-Term Actions 18, Prerule Stage 3. **47 of 129 sit at Proposed or Final
Rule Stage** — stages that by definition contemplate an NPRM or a final rule.
67% carry a real FR citation in their own timetable.

**Worked example — RIN 2127-AL99** (NHTSA, *Pilot Program for Collaborative
Research on Motor Vehicles With High or Full Driving Automation*), carrying
`None` in seven consecutive editions 202204–202510:

- `LEGAL_AUTHORITY_LIST`: `["None"]`
- `CFR_LIST`: `49 CFR 571` — and in 202204–202310 also `49 CFR 1.95`, which is
  NHTSA's **delegation-of-authority** section: an authority citation misfiled
  into the CFR field while the authority field says there is none
- Timetable: **ANPRM published 10/10/2018 at 83 FR 50872**

An agency that published an ANPRM in the Federal Register necessarily cited
authority to do it. `None` here is a defect, not a signal.

**Revised verdict.** `unstated` remains the right *parse* verdict — there is no
citation to read, and the builder is right not to count it as a failure. But
the value itself is off-form and contradicts the governing EO, so it should be
reported as a **publisher/agency data-quality defect**, not passed through as a
lawful "inapplicable". Five HHS/CMS rows (0938-AJ65, AJ83, AJ88, AJ89, AK16,
editions 200004–200010) are the extreme case: `None` for authority, `None` for
CFR, `Completed Actions`, and no FR citation at all — nothing in any field.

**Not Yet Determined (n=5,461).** A different roster: SEC 11.3%, EPA 9.8%, DOT

**None / N/A (n=129).** HHS 34.9%, EPA 23.3%, USDA 17.1%, DOT 10.1% — and

### By era

- **1995.10 – 1999.04 (8 editions).** Hard 16-element cap on both
  `LEGAL_AUTHORITY_LIST` and `CFR_LIST`, every one marked with `...`.
  352 ellipsis rows, all provably incomplete. Placeholder share of all authority
  rows: 0.18%–0.54%.
- **1999.10 (the hinge).** Cap lifts — longest list jumps from 16 to 58 in a
  single edition. The 58 mid-list ellipses cluster here and in the four
  editions after. Placeholder share begins climbing.
- **2000 – 2005 (the peak).** Placeholder share rises from 0.61% to a maximum
  of **2.32%** (200410, 200510). Ellipsis and NYD both grow.
- **2005 – 2024 (plateau).** Steady 1.6%–2.2%. Ellipsis declines slowly in
  absolute terms (222 in 200404 → 111 in 202410) while NYD holds; the mix
  shifts from ellipsis-dominant to NYD-dominant around 2008.
- **2025 (uptick).** 202504 at 2.17% and 202510 at **2.49%** — the highest
  share since 2004, driven by NYD (163 rows in 202510, the most since 2010).

carries 297 placeholder rows: 163 NYD, 131 ellipsis, 3 None/NA — led by DOT
(42), HHS (32), Commerce (19), NCUA (18), SEC (18).

## 7. What this does *not* license

**Label never guess. Nothing vanishes.** Nothing here says the builder should
synthesise a citation. It says the opposite: the ellipsis is the publisher
telling us, in a documented sentinel, that citations exist which it never
recorded — and the only correct response is to **name that**, not to fill it.

What I would propose, and what each costs:

1. **Split the label — free, no inference.** `authority_type='unstated'`
   conflates three facts the publisher deliberately distinguishes *on its own
   form*: "not yet determined" (5,461, a sanctioned checkbox), "more citations
   exist, not listed" (6,873, a different checkbox), and an off-form `None`
   (129). The form separates them; the parquet should not merge them. This is
   exactly the distinction `citation_grammar.py:922-926` already argues for
   between "said nothing" and "said something unreadable". **No new claim is
   made about what the ellipsis stands for** — the label is copied from the
   publisher's own vocabulary.

2. **Do NOT count an elision as a complete list.** Any consumer treating an
   Agenda authority list as exhaustive is wrong for 6,873 RIN-editions across
   1,671 rules, and the publisher has said so explicitly. That is a
   **completeness** claim the data cannot support and which is silently false
   today. This is the finding with real downstream consequences.

3. **Report the 129 `None` rows as a defect, not a value.** §0 shows the form
   has no such option; §4 shows 47 of them sit at Proposed or Final Rule Stage.
   Naming them costs nothing and needs no inference.

### Evidence that would have to be measured before going further

If anyone wants to *recover* what an ellipsis stands for — and I am not
recommending it:

- **(a) Does the publisher's own HTML view show more than the XML? — MEASURED:
  no.** §2.6. Independently confirmed against the printed FR Agenda.
- **(b) Is there any publisher artifact holding the omitted citations? —
  ANSWERED: no.** §0 settles this: the agency checked a box *instead of*
  entering them, so they were never in ROCIS. There is nothing to recover from
  the publisher, in any format. This closes the recovery question entirely and
  is the single most consequential thing the documentary research established.
- **(c) A hand-labelled sample, if a *rate* is ever needed.** The 447/790/826
  split in §2.3 is a corpus artifact. To claim how many citations a typical
  ellipsis stands for, you would need ~100 dotted rows read against the FR
  document each rule actually published. Note this measures the *world*, not
  the Agenda — the Agenda no longer holds the answer.
- **(d) Never** infer the omitted citations from the CFR part's authority note.
  §2.4 shows the direction of the gap, but part authority citations drift over
  decades and a rule's Agenda list is not its part's authority line (specimen
  8's list contains `16 USC 1021 to 1032`, which the current part authority
  does not). Using eCFR to *fill* the gap would mint citations the publisher
  never made. Corroboration only.

The 352 ceiling-era rows remain the one place where a claim can be made today
without inference — "more than 15 applicable authorities existed and only 15
were published" is provable from the table in §2.2 alone, and names an
incompleteness without guessing its contents.

## 8. What this report does not establish

- **Whether ROCIS hard-blocks submission on a missing Legal Authority.** The
  ROCIS Unified Agenda Module user guide and its validation-error list sit
  behind the ROCIS login. EO 12866 §4(b) requires the field and the corpus is
  empirically never empty, but I cannot say the *system* enforces it.
- **Whether the 1990s ceiling lived in the form or the export** (§2.2). No
  1990s RID form was located. The consequence — more than 15 authorities
  existed for those 352 rows — holds under either mechanism.
- **Whether the "additional citations" checkbox is stored as a boolean and
  materialised into a literal `...` row by RISC, or stored directly as a row.**
  Not documented either way. It does not affect any verdict here.
- **The 58 mid-list ellipses** (§2.5). Meaning undetermined; none since 2010.
- **Whether any of the 129 `None` rows is the one genuinely exempt document
  class** — a Rules-category document with no regulatory text (§4). Nothing in
  the Agenda record marks that distinction.
- **No source, primary or secondary, discusses Legal Authority field *content*
  quality.** The documented criticism of Agenda accuracy (GAO-01-1024R; ACUS
  Rec. 2015-1, 80 FR 36757; Copeland's 2015 ACUS draft report; O'Connell, 94
  Va. L. Rev. 889) targets dates, priority/significance flags, RFA
  determinations and missing entries — never this field. **[Inference]** this
  analysis appears to be new ground, which is a reason to hold it to a higher
  evidentiary bar, not a lower one.

### One operational hazard found along the way

The 2012 Agenda is the single edition whose filename breaks the `{YYYYMM}`
pattern: reginfo serves it as `REGINFO_RIN_DATA_2012.xml` while its internal
`PUBLICATION_ID` is `201210`. Spring 2012 was never published (confirmed by CRS
IF12118 Table 1, which lists it as "Not published"). Requesting either
`REGINFO_RIN_DATA_201204.xml` or `REGINFO_RIN_DATA_201210.xml` returns **HTTP
200 with a ~50 KB HTML disclosure page containing zero `RIN_INFO` elements** —
so a naive fetcher silently ingests an error page as an edition. The pinned
corpus already handles this correctly; a future re-pin must not regress it.

## Reproduction

Population and spellings, from the pinned parquet:

```sql
SELECT authority_text, count(*), count(DISTINCT rin),
       min(publication_id), max(publication_id)
FROM 'output/registry-real-data-sources/unified-agenda-parquet/unified_agenda_legal_authorities.parquet'
WHERE authority_type = 'unstated'
GROUP BY 1 ORDER BY 2 DESC;
```

The ceiling measurement (§2.2) reads every `<LEGAL_AUTHORITY_LIST>` in each
pinned edition XML and reports, per edition, the longest list, the number of
16-element lists, how many of those end in `...`, and the number of lists
longer than 16.

Specimen draw: population sorted by `(publication_id, rin)`, `random.seed(20260822)`,
`random.sample(population, 10)`.

Cross-edition comparisons (§2.3) rebuild each dotted RIN's full authority-list
history from all 60 pinned editions, then compare the pre-ellipsis prefix
against the longest unelided edition of the same RIN.

Every corpus figure in this report was measured against the pinned editions and
the pinned parquet only. Every documentary claim in §0 and §4 is quoted from
the sources listed above; where a question could not be answered from a source,
§8 says so rather than filling the gap.
