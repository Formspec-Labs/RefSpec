8 · The coordinate-system split — corrected, and the risk ranking flipped

The previous version of this section made a category error, and it inverted the risk ranking. This is the largest single correction on the page. It treated rkaf:coordinateSystem and coordinate_system as two spellings of one field, counted nine spellings across them, and concluded that the 4.2 M-record spelling was the scandal. They are two different fields with two different contracts, and once that is fixed the alarming number stops being alarming and a quieter one takes its place.

The risk ranking flips. Ordered by what it actually costs to fix:

  1. DocSpec's utf8-byte-range is the real hazard — 8,232,356 records, verified. Not because of the spelling but because it is an arithmetic disagreement: byte offsets and codepoint offsets are different numbers over the same text, and no rename reconciles them. Critically, DocSpec's move to bytes was deliberate, not drift — which means the fix is a negotiated decision between two designs, not a correction of a mistake. This is the one that needs a conversion, and it is blob-backed and feasible only while the content-addressed objects survive (5,990 objects, 34 MB).
  2. Codepoint-vs-codepoint spelling is cheap — unicode-codepoints-half-open vs rkaf:unicode-codepoint vs unicode-code-points all describe the same arithmetic. Respelling changes no offset. But it is digest-load-bearing: the string is inside the canonicalised payload, so respelling changes artifact IDs and invalidates every receipt taken over the old form. Cheap in logic, not free in provenance — do it once, deliberately, not incrementally.
Vocabulary authority is narrower than reported. source-fragment.cue is the sole authority. Checked this pass and confirmed empty of coordinate vocabulary: crates/rkaf-core/src/generated/concept_assignment.rs, constraints/core/evidence-binding.cue, and its compiled twin — zero hits for coordinate, codepoint, utf8-byte, utf16 or page-region in all three. The earlier reading that treated these as co-authorities overstated how distributed the vocabulary is.

What survives from the earlier reading, unchanged and still the reason this is not an emergency. No code anywhere branches on the coordinate-system string to choose byte-versus-codepoint arithmetic. Every numeric consumer hard-codes its own producer's assumption unconditionally — DocSpec always byte-slices, SpicyRegs always codepoint-slices, RuleSpec's validator only checks start > end ordering and never reads the unit, and SpicySearch validates membership in its own two local spellings rather than converting. Nothing crosses a byte/codepoint boundary. So there is no live correctness bug — there is unreconciled vocabulary that would become one the moment anything did cross.

Conversion is mechanically feasible while the inputs survive. SpicyRegs stores the literal text slice inline, so its records are convertible with no lookup. DocSpec stores a required source_digest and no text — but the content-addressed blobs for that exact corpus are still on disk (5,990 objects, 34 MB), keyed by the digest the records reference. RuleSpec's digest fields are optional, so records omitting them would be unconvertible.

Consequence for ordering, restated. The earlier version said “repoint eight of the nine producers.” With the two fields separated, most of those producers are not mis-spelling anything and need no action at all. The work is: (a) decide the byte-vs-codepoint question with DocSpec, which is a design negotiation and the only expensive item; (b) decide once whether to respell the codepoint family, accepting that artifact IDs move; (c) make the conforming producer actually produce, since today it has six records to its name.

Where two tracers characterised this differently — now resolved by the field split. One proved the incompatibility is real at the type level, compiling RuleSpec's closed enum and showing both foreign strings deserialize to Err, and called it corrupting. Another established that no consumer reads the unit at all, and called it latent. Both were reasoning about the wrong pairing. The type-level rejection is real but applies only to rkaf:coordinateSystem, where the foreign strings never appear; the strings that do appear at bulk volume live in a field with no enum to fail. The residue that survives the correction is smaller and sharper than either reading: one genuine arithmetic disagreement (8.2 M byte-ranged records), one cosmetic-but-digest-bearing spelling family, and one conforming producer that has never run.

9 · Receipts — what they attest, and what they do not

The earlier traces leaned on receipts as though a receipt were a warrant over everything it mentions. It is narrower than that, but also stronger in one specific way than the page previously credited.

The mechanism, traced. _sha256() (ontology/receipt.py:63-68) → _artifact_records() (:136, digest loop around :169) → build_receipt() (:2068), driven by main() (:2195-2246). main() takes only build arguments — it has no add_subparsers, and the string “verify” appears nowhere in the file. There is no way to ask receipt.py to check its own past work.

But the receipts hold up when you check them by hand. Re-hashing this pass found 9 of 9 generation artifacts still match their recorded digests. authority_edges.parquet at 10,618 rows still hashes to the 0bc929b6… recorded in the ontology manifest, byte-for-byte, today. The absence of a verify command is a missing convenience, not evidence of rot.

correction to this page “No code loads a receipt and re-checks its digests” is wrong. Three places do, and one is a real user-facing command:

  • src/spicy_regs/document_release_v3_verify.py:338-340,407-409 re-checks member digests and sizes — exposed as a verify subcommand at document_release_v3_cli.py:216.

The accurate narrow claim is: receipt.py itself has no verify path. Verification exists, but it is scattered across three modules with no common entry point.

The honest statement about inputs versus outputs. _file_record() (pipelines/materialized.py:64-67 — not ontology/materialized.py, which does not exist) stores bytes + sha256 and never rows. So on its own a file record is silent about cardinality.

10 · What is genuinely healthy

  • RefSpec → RuleSpec is the model edge. Verifies the target checkout's git rev-parse HEAD against a pinned evidence revision, requires a clean tree, checks the pinned revision is an ancestor, then subprocess-runs RuleSpec's real validators — all behind a required --rulespec-dir flag.
  • The RuleSpec → RefSpec closure pin is the best-engineered artifact here. Not one file digest but a 45-artifact closure — two commit revisions, twenty generated-artifact digests, plus corpus, constraint and self-certification digests.   • DocSpec's archive is staged extraction, not a fork. 588 files inherited from SpicyRegs, explicitly retired in archive.json and in commit e7b577b (“replace the active package with a dependency-inverted kernel”), and held behind three separate boundary tests — including one that inspects the built wheel for an archive path component.   • DocSpec's identity is unforgeable by construction. Blob keys are observed, not chosen; no uuid, random, secrets or token_hex anywhere in src/docspec/; floats forbidden in identity payloads; every minted URN recomputes itself in __post_init__ and raises on mismatch.

  • SpicySearch's snapshot verifier works. The broken snapshot below is that verifier doing its job.
  • DocSpec's clean-room tripwire is an inverted pin — it asserts DocSpec's code must not resemble the SpicyRegs snapshot it forked from — with a hard assert and no skip path. 4/4 pass.

13 · Corrections, disagreements, and what remains unverified

Where the 2026-08-09 extraction reviews supersede this page's own earlier version

These corrections are newer than the list below and take precedence over it. Several correct claims that this page itself asserted confidently on 2026-08-09 before the ownership constraint and the four extraction reviews were known.

  • The organizing principle was wrong. This was written as “five peer repos.” It is four repos owned by Formspec-Labs plus one, civictechdc/spicy-regs, that is not owned. Every severability judgment in §6 was priced without that constraint. See §2.
  • The reset-risk framing was inverted. The page treated output/ as the exposure; it is gitignored (.gitignore:11) and survives untouched. The exposure is docs/evidence/ — tracked, 202 files, zero on origin/main. The reset destroys the prose and leaves the data. See §4.
  • The coordinate-system section was a category error. rkaf:coordinateSystem (closed 6-value enum) and coordinate_system (open string) are different fields. The 4,249,550 records are conforming, not deviant. The risk ranking flips: DocSpec's utf8-byte-range is the hazard because it is an arithmetic disagreement and a deliberate design choice, not drift. See §8.
  • “715,080 appears in no receipt anywhere” is wrong — it is attested as rows in at least two receipts (agency-crosswalk-2026-08-02, date-event-artifact-2026-08-01). It is separately true that it also appears in commit a31605b's message body. See §9.
  • “No code loads a receipt and re-checks its digests” is wrong — three modules do, one of them behind a real verify subcommand. Only receipt.py itself lacks the path. And re-hashing found 9 of 9 generation artifacts still match. See §9.
  • authority_edges 10,618 vs 11,793 is not stale-vs-current — they are different corpora. 10,618 is receipt-backed and still matches disk; 11,793 lives only in output/discovery-slice-*, which contains no receipt at all.
  • is_most_citing is not misused — the earlier reading was wrong about that. But “zero consumers” is also wrong: spicysearch/agency_priors.py reads it and enforces its invariants, and reads cfr-part-agencies.parquet too. A SpicyRegs-scoped sweep could not see them.
  • The quarantine-larger-than-output observation was a category error — the 35,662 rows are upstream source-record defects (30,405 + 5,225 + 32), a different population from the 34,612 output rows.
