3 · What moves next — severability, cheapest first

Each off-target edge, sized by what is actually load-bearing on it.

  1. DocSpec → SpicyRegs — near-free. Already CLI-overridable: --spicyregs at fr_mirrulations_qualification.py:630, and every real use site reads args.spicyregs rather than the hardcoded default. Confined to 2 files, with zero references inside src/docspec/. Severance is changing a default.
  2. SpicyRegs → RefSpec — bounded and already being prepared. 9 production import statements across 8 files, using a fixed surface of 22 distinct symbols in 4 namespaces. The two heaviest consumers already import lazily inside functions, and five tests in tests/test_candidate_release_protocol.py monkeypatch builtins.__import__ to prove one whole path never imports refspec. That firewall is the seam being cut in advance — the work is reimplementing an enumerable 22-symbol surface, not open-ended discovery.
  3. RefSpec-as-submodule — trivial per touch point, but blocked. Functional path-hardcoding is nearly absent (3 non-functional string mentions). The real coupling is submodules: true in 15 places across 12 workflow files, needed only because the editable install requires RefSpec/ on disk. Structurally downstream of item 2 — it cannot close until that does.
  4. SpicySearch's vendored copies — quantifiable, but severance means building something new. ~142 files across 10 fixture locations, ~6.4 MB, all digest-verified. But SpicySearch has zero package imports of any sibling repo, so replacing copies with live consumption is net-new integration, not a call-site swap. The only existing seam is a test-only environment-variable fallback.
  5. RuleSpec → RefSpec — most expensive, and the cost is rising. There is no import edge to cut: a 718-line locally-written reader over a 1.4 MB vendored corpus, with 3 production consumers. The difficulty is that the format it targets was retired today, so the work is open-ended remediation against an unscoped Atlas 3.0 target rather than a bounded diff — and nothing detects the drift automatically.
The retirement that raised item 5's price. RefSpec commit 5c6d889 (2026-08-09 14:46 — 307 files changed, 20,711 deletions, 63 files deleted) removed bindings/atlas/1.0/examples/, bindings/atlas/1.0/fixtures/, all of bindings/atlas/2.0/, and build_vocabulary_atlas from the public API. Its message reads “Greenfield, no consumers.” True of RefSpec's own code paths; not true of the workspace — both consumers hold digest-pinned copies taken from exactly those paths. Nothing breaks at runtime today, because each keeps its own copy. But the upstream those digests point at no longer exists, so no copy can be re-derived or re-verified, and enabling the drift gate now raises FileNotFoundError rather than reporting drift.

5 · The coordinate-system split — the one where order matters

Why this is different from the other five. Every other duplication can be settled later by picking a winner and deleting the losers. This one is already written into emitted data, so it needs a rewrite or a conversion, and the bill grows with every run.

It is worse than reported. Not 3 vocabularies but 9 distinct literal strings across 9 producer sites in 4 of 5 repos (RefSpec is uninvolved — zero hits). A previously uncatalogued sixth spelling, "unicode-codepoints-half-open", is the single largest SpicyRegs producer at 4,249,550 records from document_release.py:43, with no cross-reference to that repo's own source.py constants. DocSpec's "utf8-byte-range" stands at 8,232,356 records, verified. SpicySearch adds two more spellings of its own.

But the price today is far lower than it looks, and this is the finding that should drive the decision. No code anywhere branches on the coordinate-system string to choose byte-versus-codepoint arithmetic. Every numeric consumer hard-codes its own producer's assumption unconditionally — DocSpec always byte-slices, SpicyRegs always codepoint-slices, RuleSpec's validator only checks start > end ordering and never reads the unit, and SpicySearch validates membership in its own two local spellings rather than converting. Nothing crosses a byte/codepoint boundary. So there is no live correctness bug — there is unreconciled vocabulary that would become one the moment anything did cross.

Conversion is mechanically feasible while the inputs survive. SpicyRegs stores the literal text slice inline, so its records are convertible with no lookup. DocSpec stores a required source_digest and no text — but the content-addressed blobs for that exact corpus are still on disk (5,990 objects, 34 MB), keyed by the digest the records reference. RuleSpec's digest fields are optional, so records omitting them would be unconvertible.

Consequence for ordering. Repointing eight of the nine producers at one spelling costs almost nothing today, because nothing downstream interprets the string. Only DocSpec's 8.2 M-record corpus needs a blob-backed conversion, and only if it must be migrated rather than grandfathered. Every day of further emission adds to that one corpus.

Where two tracers characterised this differently, unreconciled by me. One proved the incompatibility is real at the type level — it compiled RuleSpec's closed enum and showed both foreign strings deserialize to Err — and called it corrupting. Another established that no consumer currently reads the unit at all, and called it latent. Both are correct on their own evidence. The synthesis on which they agree: the vocabulary is genuinely incompatible, it is baked into 12.5 M records, and nothing is being corrupted yet.

6 · What is genuinely healthy

  • RefSpec → RuleSpec is the model edge. Verifies the target checkout's git rev-parse HEAD against a pinned evidence revision, requires a clean tree, checks the pinned revision is an ancestor, then subprocess-runs RuleSpec's real validators — all behind a required --rulespec-dir flag.
  • The RuleSpec → RefSpec closure pin is the best-engineered artifact here. Not one file digest but a 45-artifact closure — two commit revisions, twenty generated-artifact digests, plus corpus, constraint and self-certification digests. Running its audit reports “45 upstream inputs found; immutable closure pin verified.” Worth knowing how it got there: it had silently gone ten commits stale because its only test used a synthetic fixture, and it was found and repaired today.
  • DocSpec's archive is staged extraction, not a fork. 588 files inherited from SpicyRegs, explicitly retired in archive.json and in commit e7b577b (“replace the active package with a dependency-inverted kernel”), and held behind three separate boundary tests — including one that inspects the built wheel for an archive path component. That is a migration holding pen with a tripwire.
  • DocSpec's identity is unforgeable by construction. Blob keys are observed, not chosen; no uuid, random, secrets or token_hex anywhere in src/docspec/; floats forbidden in identity payloads; every minted URN recomputes itself in __post_init__ and raises on mismatch.
  • The Atlas pack pipeline. Dual content/transport digests computed in one streaming pass at generate_atlas_v3_full.py:5673, atomic publication by candidate-directory rename, and a parquet-view chain whose digest checks actually run (9/9 tests pass).
  • The atlas identity pin RuleSpec enforces raises AtlasIntegrityError at read time — proven by a live tamper test, not by reading the code.
  • SpicySearch's snapshot verifier works. The broken snapshot below is that verifier doing its job.
  • DocSpec's clean-room tripwire is an inverted pin — it asserts DocSpec's code must not resemble the SpicyRegs snapshot it forked from — with a hard assert and no skip path. 4/4 pass.

9 · Corrections, disagreements, and what remains unverified

Where this supersedes the 2026-08-08 traces

  • “Nothing reads the 2.6 GB build” is wrong. It is read inside RefSpec by parquet_view.py:606. It is unconsumed cross-repo only.
  • “One unset REFSPEC_CHECKOUT disables every cross-repo freshness check” is wrong in both directions. It gates one RuleSpec class. SpicySearch's four equivalents fall back to vendored copies and run; a fifth bypasses the variable entirely and is live.
  • The biggest dead artifact was missed — 8.7 GB of hidden orphaned build candidates against the 2.6 GB reported.
  • refspec is not importable from RuleSpec. The one apparent import is Python source inside a string executed by RefSpec's own interpreter, with the test asserting refspec is absent from RuleSpec's sys.modules before and after.
  • SpicySearch does consume RuleSpec's extrapolation release — a 769-line verifier wired into the CLI. The real gap is the missing drift test, not missing consumption.
  • The coordinate-system split is 9 spellings, not 3, and includes a previously uncatalogued 4.2 M-record producer — but nothing reads the unit, so it is latent rather than actively corrupting.
  • 715,080 appears in no receipt anywhere — only in .md files.
  • Zero value-ring statements is wrong — there is exactly one.
  • DocSpec's live src/ does have a network client (boto3 S3), and SpicySearch does make network calls (OpenAI, Google). Only the narrower claims survive.
  • Counts corrected: 21 rollups not 22; 9 materialised tables of 32 logical; 5 API routes not 4; DocSpec 67 live files not 105; ~11 ring declarations not 16; ≥12 canonical-JSON implementations not 6; 3 live document-release readers not 4; 120 cross-repo duplicate hashes not 69; DocSpec archive 598 files / 259,524 insertions.
  • Two SpicyRegs USC readers and four ontology modules have no importer at all — I had drawn the USC ingress as production.

Where tracers disagreed

  • The coordinate-system split — characterised as corrupting by one tracer (type-level rejection proven by compiling the enum) and as latent by another (no consumer reads the unit). Both stand on their own evidence; §5 states the synthesis rather than picking one.
  • test_policy_inputs_cross_repository.py — described as “actively running a live comparison” and as “skipping”. Both are right: the module is not env-gated and 3 tests pass, while the single catalog assertion is suspended by the provable tear and 1 skips.

Unverified

  • The asserted 30,889,057 quad count — not re-derived.
  • All production row counts. The canonical tables live in an R2 catalog whose credentials are absent; no network calls were made.
  • Whether RefSpec's ~53 other registry fetchers are dormant like the one traced end to end, and whether the Zyte path has ever executed.
  • Readers for two further multi-GB Atlas directories (1.4 GB and 1.8 GB), and ~35 RefSpec tools/ scripts that are neither Makefile-wired nor obviously benchmarks.
  • Whether any real content places a non-ASCII string into a canonical-JSON key — the condition that would turn the digest divergence live.
  • Whether ontology/candidate_channels.py is reached by dynamic dispatch that static tracing would miss, contradicting a comment claiming it is the implemented channel.
  • Why DocSpec's 10 k run stopped — no failure receipt, no log survives.
  • Five of the claimed sixteen ring declarations could not be located in this checkout.
  • Reachability throughout is import-graph tracing, not runtime coverage; a rare dynamic-dispatch pattern could invalidate an individual “unreached” verdict.
